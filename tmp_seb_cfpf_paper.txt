Intra-Trajectory Error Balancing and Inter-Trajectory
Feature Point Clustering for Trajectory Compression
Lei Y ang , Member , IEEE,X i nC h e n g , Y uwei Liao ,R u iL i , Member , IEEE,
and Guoqi Xie , Senior Member , IEEE
Abstract—The widespread use of locatable devices leads to a
sharp increase in the storage of trajectory data, and redundant
storage of similar trajectories wastes a large amount of storage
resources. The state-of-the-art multiple trajectory compression
algorithms are developed to strip the partial information of tra-
jectory; however, these algorithms have low compression efﬁciency
because they do not eliminate the redundancy within a single
trajectory as much as possible, as well as high time overhead
due to matching of reference sub-trajectories. In this study, we
propose a new spatio-temporal trajectory compression technique,
consisting ofintra-trajectory error balancingand inter-trajectory fea-
ture point clustering . Intra-trajectory error balancing is achieved
through retaining high score (an aggregated metric) trajectory
points (i.e., feature points). Furthermore, inter-trajectory feature
point clustering realizes the fusion of similar trajectories and ex-
tracts the commonality between trajectories. Experiments are per-
formed on ﬁve real trajectory datasets, including two road datasets,
one airline dataset, and one walking dataset. Compared with the
state-of-the-art methods, our compression technique improves the
compression ratio by an average of 24.9% under the same error,
and reduces the time overhead by at least an order of magnitude.
Index Terms —Compression algorithm, similar trajectory,
spatio-temporal data.

Generally, trajectories have a predetermined destination, and
if such a trajectory occurs with a certain frequency, they are
usually similar. These trajectories are dominant in urban land
navigation, such as buses and vans on ﬁxed routes, commut-
ing routes for workers, and so on. Similarly, in unconstrained
environments like air and water navigation, moving objects
follow long and consistent paths, and different objects may

trajectories in their entirety can be cumbersome, as it requires
signiﬁcant storage costs. Fortunately, such data often contains
a lot of redundant information, which provides an opportunity
for compression. Therefore, it is a critical issue to reduce the
storage overhead of trajectory data by exploiting intra-trajectory
and inter-trajectory redundancy while ensuring its availability.
The Single Trajectory Compression (STC) algorithm is com-
monly used to simplify trajectories based on an error metric [8],
[9], [10], [11]. Given an error threshold, the trajectory points ex-
ceeding the threshold are retained as feature points. This ensures
that the maximum error of the simpliﬁed trajectory is no greater
than the error threshold while minimizing its length, which
is called the Min-Size problem [12], [13]. However, a single
error metric makes it difﬁcult for these algorithms to guarantee
information loss in other features, resulting in disproportionate
information loss.
The Multiple Trajectory Compression (MTC) algorithm ex-
tracts the commonality between similar trajectories to further
reduce storage costs [14]. TrajStore is an adaptive trajectory
storage system [15] and it reduces redundancy between tra-
jectories by time mapping technique, that is, sharing spatial
information among similar trajectories and storing only the
time mapping values of the reference trajectory. However, the
compression gains brought by the time mapping technique are
relatively low. To improve the compression ratio, the Similarity-
based MTC (SMTC) algorithm [16] performs optimal storage
based on the STC algorithm and the time mapping technology
with all compressed trajectories as reference, which improves
the compression ratio and also brings high time complexity.
REference-based Spatio-Temporal compression (REST) [17],
[18] is the latest MTC technology, which compresses trajec-
tories by constructing a large set of reference sub-trajectories.
However, REST appears to prioritize commonalities between

B. Our Contributions
In this study, we propose a new spatio-temporal trajectory
compression technique, consisting of intra-trajectory error bal-
ancing and inter-trajectory feature point clustering. The contri-
butions of the proposed technique are summarized as follows.
1) For intra-trajectory error balancing, we propose a Score-
based Error Balancing (SEB) algorithm to eliminate re-
dundancy within the trajectory. SEB adopts the idea of
divide and conquer to solve the Min-Error problem and
unbalanced information loss that commonly exists in the
STC algorithm.
2) For inter-trajectory feature point clustering, we propose
a Cluster-based Feature Point Fusion (CFPF) algorithm
by utilizing the cluster distribution characteristics of the
feature points in similar trajectories to eliminate redun-
dancy between trajectories. CFPF reduces the granularity
of clustering from trajectory segments to feature points
and provides a solution to the performance bottleneck
problem faced by the current MTC algorithms.
3) Extensive experiments based on ﬁve real trajectory
datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed
technique. Compared with the state-of-the-art methods,
our compression technique improves the compression ra-
tio by an average of 24.9% under the same error, and re-
duces the time overhead by at least an order of magnitude.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section II
reviews related work. Sections III and IV propose the SEB
and CFPF algorithms, respectively. Section V veriﬁes the
performance of the presented SEB and CFPF algorithms, and
Section VI concludes this study.
II. R ELA TEDWORK
In this section, we review related algorithms for trajectory
compression. According to whether the redundancy between tra-
jectories is eliminated, it can be divided into two categories: sin-
gle trajectory compression and multiple trajectory compression.
A. Single Trajectory Compression
Trajectory simpliﬁcation technology is the primary form of
eliminating intra-trajectory redundancy. According to the error
metrics, it can be divided into position-preserving, direction-
preserving, and speed-preserving trajectory simpliﬁcation algo-
rithms. Since the distance is the most intuitive measure between
points, most studies aim to simplify trajectories by distance-
based error metrics, thereby preserving the location informa-
tion of trajectories [8], [9], [19], [20], [21]. Douglas–Peucker
(DP) [8] is a heuristic algorithm that ensures the maximum
Perpendicular euclidean Distance (PED) error of the simpli-
ﬁed trajectory is limited within a given threshold. Top-Down
Time-Ratio (TD-TR) [9] fully considers the spatio-temporal
characteristics on the basis of DP , and adopts Synchronous
euclidean Distance (SED) as a metric. DP and TD-TR are widely
praised for their high-quality simpliﬁcation [22]. Recently,
various extensions of the DP algorithm have been proposed.
These include integrating speed features to preserve essential
motion characteristics [23], introducing adaptive compression
thresholds with feature point selection to enhance trajectory
ﬁdelity [24], and determining thresholds based on area differ-
ence between the original and simpliﬁed trajectories [25].T o
achieve real-time compression, sliding window techniques are
also used in trajectory simpliﬁcation [11], [26], [27], [28], [29],
[30], [31]. Since speed and direction are also crucial character-
istics of moving objects, corresponding speed-preserving [32]
or direction-preserving trajectory simpliﬁcation algorithms are
developed [6], [10], [12], [33]. These advancements in single-
trajectory compression techniques improve the efﬁciency of
trajectory-based applications by reducing data size while main-
taining key motion characteristics, with speciﬁc applications
in areas such as road network analysis [21], [32] and vessel
navigation pattern recognition [24], [25].
Although the trajectory simpliﬁcation techniques mentioned
above exhibit high compression performance, most of them
rely on their own chosen error metrics, which leads to a large
loss of information on other features [13]. In fact, when using
decompressed trajectory data for analysis, the information loss
should be comprehensive rather than performing well in one
aspect and performing poorly in other aspects.
B. Multiple Trajectory Compression
Compared to the STC algorithm, the MTC algorithm further
reduces storage overhead by eliminating inter-trajectory redun-
dancy [14] and enhances location-based services by exploit-
ing inter-trajectory correlations, which further improves data

processing efﬁciency and enables more accurate analysis [15],
[16], [17], [18], [34], [35]. TrajStore [15] performs trajectory
clustering by calculating the similarity between trajectories.
Trajectories with high similarity are grouped, and a reference
trajectory is designated for each group. All trajectory points of
the reference trajectory are stored in full. For the spatial data of
the non-reference trajectory within the group, its time mapping
value on the reference trajectory is used instead. Trajic [34]
improves on TrajStore’s encoding method, but still uses the same
principle of multiple trajectory compression. SMTC [16] com-
bines the STC algorithm and time mapping to reduce redundancy
by storing time pairs in similar trajectories. However, the time
mapping technology has a large time overhead for MTC, and
also introduces the problem of reference dependence.
REST [17], [18] is a data-driven framework that com-
presses trajectories by constructing a large set of reference
sub-trajectories. However, it overlooks the redundancy of the
trajectory itself. In actual compression, matching reference tra-
jectories entail a considerable time overhead, and achieving a
higher compression ratio demands a large error threshold.
In addition to reference-based clustering, another method
exists that leverages incremental computation and motion trend
modeling to assess the importance of trajectory points between
trajectories, effectively eliminating redundant data while pre-
serving essential information. However, retaining key data can
be difﬁcult when dealing with extreme anomalies (e.g., sharp
turns) or complex motion patterns (e.g., oscillations or slow
motion) [35].
III. I NTRA-TRAJECTORY ERROR BALANCING
The main challenge of simplifying a single trajectory is how to
determine whether a trajectory point belongs to a feature point or
a redundant point. Existing trajectory simpliﬁcation algorithms
rely on a single error metric, which often results in polarized
feature losses [13]. Therefore, we propose a method to remove
redundancy within the trajectory. This improves the performance
of the algorithm on the comprehensive error and the practical
application ability. Sections III-A, III-B, and III-C introduce the
deﬁnition of the score, the resolution of the Min-Error problem,
and the implementation details of the algorithm, respectively.

used in this study.

Min-Size problem, handles this situation well [12].

C. The SEB Algorithm
In this section, we propose the SEB algorithm (Algorithm 1)
to balance the error caused by trajectory simpliﬁcation and solve
the Min-Error problem. We use the idea of divide and conquer
to ﬁnd the trajectory points with the local highest score, and use
the priority queue to obtain the trajectory points with the global
highest score as feature points. The speciﬁc description of the
SEB algorithm is as follows.
Algorithm 1: The SEB Algorithm.
1) In Lines 1-4, the priority queue PQ , the reduced trajectory
T′and the size m ofT′are initialized.P1 andPn must be
retained in T′because they represent the start and end of
the trajectory.
2) In Lines 5-12, for the remainingm−2 feature points, SEB
initially takes the entire trajectory as a segment and looks
for the highest local score trajectory point in the segment.
SEB calculates the PED, SED, SAD, and DAD errors of
each trajectory point in the segment by (2)-(6), and then
performs transformation by (7). Finally,W is weighted to
ﬁnd the trajectory point Ptarget with the highest score in
the segment. Otherwise, it takes the ﬁrst element from the
priority queue PQ toPtarget.
3) In Lines 14-20, Ptarget is put into T′as the feature point
with the highest global score. Next, the trajectory segment
is divided into two new sub-segments by Ptarget. Then,
the scores of trajectory points in two sub-segments are
updated, and the obtained two trajectory points with the
highest local score are put into the PQ .
The SEB algorithm performs trajectory simpliﬁcation accord-
ing to a given storage budget. For a trajectory with N trajectory
points and a storage budget of ω, a total of ω ×N iterations are
required. The time complexity of updating the score for each
iteration is O(N). The priority queue stores up to ω ×N −1
elements and the time complexity of adding or removing an
element each time is log(ω ×N). Therefore, the time com-
plexity of the SEB algorithm is O(ω ×N 2 ×log(ω ×N)) =
O(M ×N ×logM), where M = ω ×N represents the num-
ber of feature points to be stored.

IV . INTER-TRAJECTORY FEA TUREPOINT CLUSTERING
AND FUSION
This section describes how we extract commonality between
similar trajectories by reducing the granularity of clustering
from trajectory segments to feature points. This approach breaks
through the performance bottleneck of the existing MTC al-
gorithms, reduces the similarity requirements of multiple tra-
jectories, and avoids the problem of trajectory dependence.
Sections IV -A, IV -B, and IV -Cintroduce the core idea, the error
accumulation problem, and the implementation details of the
algorithm, respectively.
A. Core Idea
The starting point of our algorithm is the cluster characteris-
tics exhibited by the feature points of similar trajectories. For the
8 similar trajectories (after SEB simpliﬁcation) given in Fig. 3,
the feature points in the cluster are very close in spatial position.
Therefore, clustering the feature points of multiple similar tra-

C. The CFPF Algorithm
In this section, we propose the CFPF algorithm (Algorithm 2)
to extract commonalities among multiple similar trajectories,
thereby further reducing the storage overhead. The speciﬁc
description of the CFPF algorithm is as follows.
1) In Lines 4–7, each trajectory in the TG is simpliﬁed
using the SEB algorithm. Then the feature points in each
simpliﬁed trajectory are treated as a separate cluster and
added to ClusterSet .
2) In Lines 11–15, CFPF calculates the distance between two
clusters by (9), and compares it with the revised distance
threshold to determine whether clusters need to be merged.
The more effective feature points in a cluster, the higher
the compression beneﬁt.

The CFPF algorithm demonstrates high computational efﬁ-

detailed analysis of its time and space complexity. The algorithm
consists of three major steps: trajectory simpliﬁcation, feature
point clustering and fusion, and encoding.
In the time complexity analysis, the trajectory simpliﬁcation
step simpliﬁes K trajectories using the SEB algorithm with a
complexity ofO(KMN logM), whereN is the average length
of each original trajectory, and M is the average length of
each simpliﬁed trajectory ( M ≪ N ). The feature point clus-
tering, fusion, and encoding steps process the simpliﬁed trajec-
tories with a complexity of O(K2˜M 2 +log(KM)). Conse-
quently, the overall time complexity of the CFPF algorithm is
O(KMN logM +K2˜M 2 +log(KM)).
In the space complexity analysis, the primary storage require-
ment involves maintaining feature point group information, with
a complexity ofO(KM). To further optimize storage, Huffman
encoding compresses redundant data using an (l,v) format,
where l represents the length of the information and v is the
encoded value. These techniques signiﬁcantly reduce storage
overhead while maintaining computational efﬁciency.

TrajStore, SMTC, TRR-GSC, and CFPF algorithms from two
aspects: algorithm execution time and trajectory recovery time.
Fig. 16 shows that the execution time of our CFPF algorithm on
the ﬁve datasets is much lower than the other three methods.
TrajStore clusters each trajectory segment, and then maps
the spatial information of the non-reference trajectory to the
timestamp of the corresponding position of the reference tra-
jectory. Time mapping needs to traverse the reference trajectory
again and again, which brings a certain time overhead. The total
time complexity of TrajStore is O(C1 ×K2 ×N 2), where C1
is a constant, K is the number of trajectories, and N is the
length of the original trajectory. It is worth noting that in the
Berlin_0.05 dataset, the time overhead of TrajStore increases
with the increase of the compression ratio after the compression
ratio is greater than 150. A reasonable explanation is that as the
distance threshold increases, more and more trajectory points are
considered time-mappable points by TrajStore, which requires
additional traversal of the reference trajectory.
SMTC traverses all compressed trajectories to ﬁnd the best
reference trajectory and time mapping values, and then performs
single trajectory compression to select the scheme with the high-
est compression ratio. This not only brings high time overhead,
but also has the problem of trajectory dependence. The total
time complexity of SMTC is O(C2 ×K2 ×N 2), where C2 is
a relatively large constant because it involves multiple query
operations of compressed trajectories. From the ﬁve datasets, it
can be seen that SMTC has at most 10 times higher time overhead
than TrajStore and TRR-GSC, and hundreds of times higher
time overhead than CFPF. The time overhead of TRR-GSC is
mainly reﬂected in the sub-trajectory matching of the reference
set and the original trajectory. Due to the uncertainty of the set
of reference sub-trajectories, it is difﬁcult to accurately calculate
the time complexity of TRR-GSC. It can be seen from Fig. 16
that the time complexity of TRR-GSC should be between
TrajStore and SMTC, which is also a higher level. Compared
to these algorithms, CFPF clusters and fuses a small number of
feature points retained after the SEB algorithm, without recip-
rocating traversal of other trajectories. Therefore, the amount
of data is greatly reduced. Therefore, the time overhead of
CFPF is extremely low, and it does not have the problem of
reference trajectory and trajectory dependence. The total time
complexity of CFPF is O(C ×K ×M ×N ×logM), where
C is a constant and M(M ≪ N) is the length of the simpliﬁed
trajectory.
In Table V, the trajectory recovery time of CFPF on the ﬁve
datasets is signiﬁcantly lower than TrajStore, TRR-GSC, and
SMTC. The CFPF algorithm replaces the spatial information
of the feature points in the same cluster with the center point.
There is no need to ﬁnd the corresponding spatial information
according to the reference trajectory and time mapping value
when recovering the trajectory. Therefore, CFPF greatly reduces
the trajectory recovery time.

E. Case Study
To intuitively demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed
trajectory compression method, we conducted a case study using
a representative dataset of real-world aircraft trajectories from
the Airline dataset. This dataset was selected due to its clear
spatial distribution, signiﬁcant variations in heading, and the
presence of multiple turning points, which make it particularly
suitable for evaluating the performance of compression algo-
rithms in preserving essential trajectory features. As illustrated
in Fig. 17, the original trajectories (depicted as blue dots) consist
of 9 ﬂight paths, each comprising approximately 200 sampling
points. After applying the proposed compression algorithm, each
trajectory (shown as red dots) is reduced to about 70 points,
successfully retaining the main shape and critical turning points,
which are highlighted by yellow circles. Table IV summarizes
the number of trajectory points before and after compression,
as well as key error metrics such as PED, SED, Speed error,
Direction error and DTW distance. The results indicate that the
proposed method achieves a substantial reduction in data volume
while effectively preserving the fundamental characteristics and
geometric features of the original trajectories.
VI. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, we develop a new spatio-temporal trajec-
tory compression technique, consisting of intra-trajectory error
balancing and inter-trajectory feature point clustering. Intra-
trajectory error balancing is implemented by the presented
SEB algorithm, and inter-trajectory feature point clustering is
achieved through proposing CFPF algorithm. SEB balances
the error of compressed trajectories by retaining high score
trajectory points and provides an efﬁcient solution to the Min-
Error problem. CFPF reduces the granularity of clustering from
trajectory segments to feature points, and extracts the com-
monality between similar trajectories by clustering and fusing
feature point sequences. The combination of SEB and CFPF
perfectly achieves intra-trajectory and inter-trajectory redun-
dancy elimination. Extensive experiments based on ﬁve real
trajectory datasets conﬁrm that our technique performs better
than state-of-the-art algorithms in terms of compression ratio,
compression error, and time overhead. Our technique is efﬁcient
for different types (e.g., urban roads, aircraft routes, and walk-
ing) of spatio-temporal trajectory data.
